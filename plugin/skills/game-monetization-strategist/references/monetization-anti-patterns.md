# Monetization anti-patterns

Most common monetization-strategy failures. Recognize each *before* strategy ships.

## 1. Pay-to-win in skill-driven PvP

Team ships pay-affecting content in PvP game pitched on skill. Community detects within days. Trust drops; review scores tank; competitions refuse to feature game.

**Avoid by:** explicit cosmetic-only commitment in PvP modes. Progression-affecting IAP → segregate to PvE or non-competitive modes.

## 2. Cosmetic IAP in single-player

Cosmetic IAP in primarily single-player game. Conversion dramatically below F2P benchmarks. Players don't buy cosmetics nobody sees.

**Avoid by:** match IAP type to social context. Single-player → no cosmetics; instead expansions, story content, premium currency for utility.

## 3. Premium-with-required-live-ops

Game ships premium ($60 one-time) but design requires live-ops content cadence. Either team can't sustain content (game decays) or content sold separately (players feel double-billed).

**Avoid by:** match model to design. Live ops required → ship with sub or season pass *in addition to* premium, or shift to F2P.

## 4. Subscription on a finite experience

Subscription on game with clear endpoint. Players cancel after completing content. LTV unsustainable; math doesn't pencil.

**Avoid by:** subscriptions need ongoing value. Finite game → premium. Service-shaped design → sub works.

## 5. Token-source-dominated economy

Web3 game: abundant token sources, weak sinks. Token price collapses; earnings devalue; new players see no ROI; population leaves.

**Avoid by:** sinks before sources. Model token velocity in spreadsheet seriously. Plan re-tunes on velocity drift.

## 6. NFT'd combat-effective items

Game NFTs items affecting combat outcomes. Balance changes required (meta calcifies). Owners revolt at nerfs. Meta stays broken or trust destroyed.

**Avoid by:** NFT cosmetics, identity, collection items. Combat-effective items stay in web2 storage.

## 7. Dark patterns that damage trust

- **Hidden costs:** "play 3 days, then suddenly everything is gated"
- **Confused currencies:** multiple soft currencies make value comparison impossible
- **Manipulated scarcity:** "sale ends in 23:59:58" timer resetting daily
- **Pay-to-skip-pain-the-design-introduced:** design adds friction so IAP removes it ("make timer waits annoying so players pay to skip")

Short-term wins, burns audience. Long-term: studio reputation drops; press / influencers stop covering; UA channels deplatform.

**Avoid by:** transparent monetization. Players see what they pay for, why, and alternatives. IAP *makes experience better*, doesn't *remove pain design created to sell IAP*.

## 8. Whale dependence without depth

Model relies on whales, offers no aspirational endgame. Top spenders cap out within months; revenue collapses; team panics into ill-advised "whale-only" content breaking rest of game.

**Avoid by:** real endgame, multiple aspirational tracks. Status, mastery, completion, identity, social — whales need places to spend at their pace.

## 9. Battle pass fatigue

Back-to-back battle passes, similar structure. Players burn out; pass conversion drops; primary monetization vehicle decays.

**Avoid by:**
- Vary pass themes / mechanics across seasons
- Build in breaks (1–2 week gap between seasons)
- Reward returning pass-buyers with carry-over credits
- Free-track rewards genuinely useful, not thin path

## 10. Ad over-reliance + IDFA-style shock

90%+ revenue from ads. Platform policy change drops eCPM 40%. Model collapses; no fallback.

**Avoid by:** diversification. Even ad-dominant models need small IAP / sub option. Ad networks shift → team has path forward.

## 11. Crypto-as-feature

Web3 added because "everyone is doing it", no design reason. Token / NFT mechanics feel bolted on. Crypto-curious find it shallow; mass-market find it confusing. No segment well-served.

**Avoid by:** web3 must serve fantasy or verb (per `game-concept-creator`). If not, drop it. Crypto-as-marketing rarely durable.

## 12. Silent nerf

Monetized item nerfed without comms. Paying players find out via gameplay. Refund storm; community erupts; trust takes months to rebuild.

**Avoid by:** announce in advance, compensate, grandfather where possible. Coordinate with `game-marketer` for comms.

## 13. KPI-floor blind launch

Team ships globally without soft launch or with insufficient validation. KPIs below (untested) floors. UA spend wasted; re-tune scramble that belonged pre-launch.

**Avoid by:** soft launch with floor-target-strong gates. Don't ship globally on optimism.

## 14. Discount erosion

Premium game discounted aggressively at first sale. Players learn to wait. Future sales drop. Long-tail revenue collapses.

**Avoid by:**
- Hold full price first 6–12 months
- Bundles / DLC for value plays, not raw discounts
- Sales are events, not default state

## 15. Mid-tier vacuum

Catalog: $0.99 entry, $99.99 whale tier, nothing between. Dolphins (bulk of payers) have no fitting SKU; don't convert.

**Avoid by:** populate price-tier ladder: $4.99 / $9.99 / $19.99 / $49.99. Each tier ≥1 compelling SKU.

## 16. The "monetization-decorated" design

Monetization added at design end as decoration ("we'll add a battle pass and some IAP"). Each addition fights design or feels bolted-on. Frankenstein model.

**Avoid by:** monetization is part of design. Model decided alongside systems, not after. Coordinate `game-systems-designer` ↔ `game-monetization-strategist` early.

## 17. Refund flood from a broken release

Buggy release + aggressive refund policy collapses near-term revenue. Players lose patience; reviews drop; future launches face skepticism.

**Avoid by:** quality bar before release; staged rollout; honest acknowledgment; quick patch cadence; transparent comms.

## 18. The "trust break" that takes a year to recover

Monetization misstep (silent nerf, dark pattern, regulatory miss) damages community trust. Subsequent launches face headwinds even when well-designed. Trust takes 12+ months to rebuild.

**Avoid by:** invest in trust contract. Transparent monetization. Generous compensation when things go wrong. Studio reputation is part of every game's UA budget.
